# Behavioral and Leadership Question Bank

Use these questions to test the [eight-story bank](./story-bank.md). The goal is not to prepare a separate scripted answer for every question. Select the best core story, change the emphasis, and answer the exact question asked.

## B-01 — Technical challenge and complexity

1. Tell me about the most technically challenging project you worked on.
2. What is the most complex system you have personally helped build?
3. Describe a difficult technical problem that took several attempts to solve.
4. Tell me about a project where you had to learn an unfamiliar technology quickly.
5. Describe a problem where the obvious solution did not work.
6. What technical accomplishment are you most proud of?
7. Tell me about a project with difficult constraints or dependencies.
8. Describe a time you simplified a complicated system.
9. How did you divide a large technical problem into manageable pieces?
10. Tell me about a time your technical depth materially changed an outcome.

## B-02 — Architecture, judgment, and trade-offs

1. Tell me about an important architecture decision you made.
2. Describe a time you chose between two reasonable technical approaches.
3. Tell me about a short-term versus long-term trade-off you made.
4. Describe a time you intentionally accepted technical debt.
5. Tell me about a build-versus-buy decision.
6. Describe a decision you made with incomplete technical information.
7. Tell me about an architecture proposal you changed after receiving new evidence.
8. How have you balanced reliability, delivery speed, cost, and maintainability?
9. Tell me about a design that worked initially but later needed to evolve.
10. Describe a time you prevented unnecessary over-engineering.
11. Tell me about a technical standard or pattern you introduced.
12. How did you validate that an architecture decision was successful?

## B-03 — Incidents, reliability, and pressure

1. Tell me about the most serious production incident you handled.
2. Describe a time you debugged a problem under significant pressure.
3. Tell me about an outage where the cause was not immediately clear.
4. How did you prioritize mitigation versus root-cause analysis?
5. Describe a time you had to communicate bad news during an incident.
6. Tell me about a deployment or migration that went wrong.
7. Describe a recurring operational problem you permanently eliminated.
8. Tell me about a time monitoring or alerting failed to detect the real problem.
9. How have you improved an on-call or incident-response process?
10. What did your team change after a major incident?
11. Tell me about a time rollback was difficult or impossible.
12. Describe a controlled failure or resilience test that exposed an important risk.

## B-04 — Performance, scale, and efficiency

1. Tell me about a performance problem you diagnosed and fixed.
2. Describe a system you helped scale for significant growth.
3. Tell me about a database query or data model you optimized.
4. Describe a time you reduced latency or increased throughput.
5. Tell me about a memory, CPU, connection-pool, or thread-pool problem.
6. How did you prove where the bottleneck was?
7. Describe a caching decision and its trade-offs.
8. Tell me about a time you reduced infrastructure or operational cost.
9. Describe a performance improvement that introduced a new risk.
10. How did you establish a baseline and measure the final result?

## B-05 — Disagreement, conflict, and influence

1. Tell me about a disagreement with another engineer.
2. Describe a time you disagreed with your manager or technical lead.
3. Tell me about a technical proposal of yours that was rejected.
4. Describe a time you changed your mind after a disagreement.
5. Tell me about a conflict between engineering and product priorities.
6. How have you influenced a decision without having formal authority?
7. Describe a time stakeholders wanted incompatible outcomes.
8. Tell me about a difficult code-review or design-review discussion.
9. Describe a time you had to say no or push back on a deadline.
10. Tell me about a teammate who was difficult to work with.
11. How did you handle a decision you disagreed with after it was made?
12. Tell me about a time you built consensus across teams.

## B-06 — Ambiguity, ownership, and execution

1. Tell me about an ambiguous project you drove.
2. Describe a time requirements were unclear or frequently changing.
3. Tell me about a project where no one clearly owned the problem.
4. How did you decide what to do first when everything seemed important?
5. Describe a time you identified a problem before anyone assigned it to you.
6. Tell me about a project with several external dependencies.
7. Describe a time you created structure for a disorganized effort.
8. Tell me about a project where you had to make progress with missing data.
9. How have you managed risks in a long-running project?
10. Describe a time priorities changed midway through delivery.
11. Tell me about a project you took from idea to production.
12. How did you keep stakeholders aligned during an uncertain project?

## B-07 — Failure, learning, and self-awareness

1. Tell me about a significant mistake you made.
2. Describe a project that failed or missed its goals.
3. Tell me about a deadline you missed.
4. Describe a decision you would make differently today.
5. Tell me about critical feedback you received.
6. Describe a time you underestimated complexity or risk.
7. Tell me about a time your assumptions were wrong.
8. Describe a time you introduced a defect or regression.
9. Tell me about a time you failed to communicate effectively.
10. What is a professional weakness you are actively improving?
11. Tell me about a time you changed a strongly held technical opinion.
12. What process or behavior changed permanently because of a failure?

## B-08 — Mentoring, leadership, and cross-team impact

1. Tell me about someone you mentored.
2. Describe a time you helped another engineer become more effective.
3. Tell me about a project where you acted as a technical leader.
4. How have you raised engineering quality across a team?
5. Describe a time you delegated an important responsibility.
6. Tell me about a time you gave difficult but useful feedback.
7. Describe a time you received feedback and changed your leadership approach.
8. How have you helped onboard a new engineer?
9. Tell me about a cross-team initiative you influenced.
10. Describe a time you made other teams more successful.
11. How have you balanced delivering your work with helping teammates?
12. Tell me about a process, tool, or standard you established for others.
13. Describe a time you recognized and developed someone’s strengths.
14. What does senior engineering leadership mean without people management?

## Career narrative and motivation

These answers do not always need a full STAR story, but they must remain consistent with the resume and story bank.

1. Walk me through your background.
2. Tell me about yourself.
3. Why are you considering a change now?
4. Why are you interested in this company and role?
5. What type of problems do you want to solve next?
6. What are you looking for in your next team and manager?
7. What is the common thread across your career decisions?
8. Why did you leave each previous role?
9. What distinguishes you as a senior engineer?
10. Where do you want your career to develop over the next few years?

## Follow-up probes to practice for every story

Interviewers often test whether a polished opening answer is supported by real experience. Practice these probes for every story:

1. What exactly did you personally do?
2. What did other team members own?
3. What alternatives did you consider?
4. Why did you reject the strongest alternative?
5. What was the scale before and after?
6. How did you measure the result?
7. What was the largest risk?
8. Who disagreed, and why?
9. What unexpected problem occurred?
10. What would you do differently now?
11. How did customers or the business benefit?
12. What happened after you moved on?

## Practice method

1. Pick one question randomly.
2. Take no more than 30 seconds to select a story and structure the answer.
3. Give a two-minute answer aloud.
4. Answer two random follow-up probes without notes.
5. Record any missing fact, weak metric, or unclear personal contribution in the story bank.
6. Repeat the answer later using a different question from the same family.
