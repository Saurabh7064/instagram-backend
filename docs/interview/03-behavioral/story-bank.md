# Eight-Story Behavioral Bank

These stories live in the behavioral folder because the interviewer is evaluating ownership, judgment, communication, and impact. For stories B-01 through B-04, link to supporting architecture and metrics in [Resume and Project Deep-Dives](../09-resume-project-deep-dives/README.md).

Do not write a different story for every interview question. Prepare these eight stories deeply, then adapt the emphasis to the question.

## B-01 — Most technically challenging project

Show technical depth, how you decomposed complexity, your direct contribution, and why the solution mattered. Do not spend the whole answer describing the team’s architecture.

## B-02 — Architecture or design decision

Show options, decision criteria, disagreement or uncertainty, the chosen trade-off, and how you validated the decision. Avoid presenting the final choice as obvious.

## B-03 — Major production incident

Show calm prioritization, mitigation, diagnosis, communication, recovery, and prevention. Separate the immediate fix from the systemic corrective actions.

## B-04 — Performance or scalability improvement

Establish the baseline with evidence, explain the bottleneck, describe experiments, and quantify the improvement. Include cost or complexity trade-offs.

## B-05 — Disagreement with another engineer

Show that you listened, clarified the actual decision criteria, used evidence, and preserved the working relationship. The goal is sound resolution, not proving that you won.

## B-06 — Ambiguous project you drove

Show how you converted uncertainty into requirements, milestones, risks, owners, and feedback loops. Explain how you moved forward without pretending uncertainty had disappeared.

## B-07 — Failure, mistake, or changed opinion

Choose a real, meaningful mistake. Own your part without blaming circumstances, explain the consequence, and show the specific behavior or system that changed afterward.

## B-08 — Mentoring, leadership, or cross-team influence

Show influence beyond assigned code: raising another engineer’s effectiveness, aligning teams, improving a process, or driving a decision without relying on authority.

## Story selection rules

- Prefer examples from the last three to five years unless an older story is uniquely strong.
- Reuse a project across stories only when the decision points and demonstrated competencies are genuinely different.
- Include at least one story where the outcome was imperfect.
- Never invent metrics. Use truthful ranges or qualitative outcomes when exact numbers are unavailable.
- Remove confidential names, customer data, credentials, and proprietary implementation details.
